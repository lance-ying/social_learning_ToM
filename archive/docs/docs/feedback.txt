The formal content is solid, but for a journal like Cognition you're writing for a broader cognitive science audience, many of whom won't be steeped in the POMDPs and inverse planning literature. A few areas where I think more context would help:
Opening the section. Your current intro paragraph jumps straight into "partially-observable multiagent setting" without giving the reader an intuitive sense of what the model is trying to do in plain language. Before the formalism, it would help to have a short paragraph that lays out the conceptual logic: the observer faces a choice at every moment between acting toward their own goal or pausing to watch someone else, and the value of watching depends on what the observer thinks the other agent knows, wants, and will do next. That framing gives readers a mental scaffold to hang the equations on.
The forward model. You introduce equations 1 through 5 as a block, but you don't say much about what each component means in the context of your task. For instance, what does the belief update look like concretely? In your game, beliefs are presumably about which wizard holds the amulet. Stating that explicitly would help readers connect the formalism to the experimental setup. Similarly, the Q-function is introduced as "the estimated cost of the optimal plan," but it's worth a sentence explaining that this is computed via A* search over the grid, which you mention at the very end but could introduce earlier so readers aren't wondering how Q is computed while reading the equations.
The utility estimation. This is the core contribution, but the transition from equation 7 to equation 8 moves quickly. A few things that could use more unpacking: you say "we assume the agent's observing is optimal where they stop observing when the benefit of additional observations doesn't offset the observation costs," but this is doing a lot of work.
